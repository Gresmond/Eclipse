Privacy

Eclipse has no accounts or database of its own. Your rituals, tasks and journal stay in your browser and your own Google Drive.

What Eclipse asks Google for

Your email address and Google account ID, to tell whose data this is. A hidden folder in your Drive that only Eclipse can see, for syncing and daily backups. Files Eclipse makes itself, for the Eclipse Journals folder holding a readable copy of your journal. Eclipse can’t see anything else in your Drive.

What passes through Eclipse’s server

Google’s sign-in has to finish on a server. Eclipse’s server encrypts your lasting sign-in, your email address and your account ID into a cookie kept by your browser, and keeps no copy. Your browser sends that cookie back to get an hour’s access to your Drive. Your rituals, tasks and journal never pass through the server: your browser talks to Drive directly.

What’s kept in your browser

A copy of your data, so Eclipse works offline, and the cookie above. Signing out syncs, then erases both.

Other services

Eclipse is hosted on Cloudflare Pages, which handles requests to this site, and uses Google Drive for storage. There are no ads or analytics, and your data isn’t sold or shared.

Eclipse’s use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Taking your data back

Sign out to erase this browser’s copy. To remove what’s in Drive, delete the Eclipse Journals folder, and remove Eclipse in Drive’s settings under Manage apps, choosing to delete its hidden data. To end its access, go to your Google account’s connections.
